## 1. Frontier Labs

- [Gemini 4 Argon](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/) [article] — Google DeepMind's new flagship targets software engineering, science, robotics and professional workflows, with a 1M-token output window. TLDR says it is broadly available. Other sources I found say it was announced Sept 30 with no public endpoint, so confirm before committing. **Why it matters:** it is a third frontier option for model-routing and fallback conversations with clients, and the 1M output window suits long agentic tasks.
- [Meta Muse Wonder](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-wonder) [article] — Meta's first frontier-class models (Wonder and Wonder Full). They add "active memory", are strong on coding, and run through the Meta Model API and Muse Code. **Why it matters:** another API vendor to price against Anthropic and OpenAI. Active memory is a pattern to track for stateful enterprise agents.
- [Anthropic quietly tests a new model under Claude Opus 5.5](https://www.testingcatalog.com/anthropic-tests-new-model-opus-5-5-routing-opus-5-6-fable-5-5/) [article] — Users saw Opus 5.5 outputs well above its usual level over the weekend, which suggests traffic was routed to a new checkpoint. **Why it matters for SaidnDone:** the model behind a fixed ID can shift, so keep evals and pinned-model regression tests running. Separately, TLDR Dev reports Anthropic told investors Q3 revenue rose 45% to $16.8B ahead of a possible IPO (no primary link in the newsletter).

## 2. Frontier Research

- [How close are AI models to self-replication?](https://www.anthropic.com/research/self-replicating-ai) [article] — Anthropic tests current models on autonomous self-copying across consumer hardware, from single steps to end-to-end runs. **Why it matters:** it is a concrete example of the dangerous-capability evals clients' risk teams will ask about, and good material for an AI PM safety-framing answer.
- [Can we change a model's architecture without retraining from scratch? ("Priming")](https://www.amazon.science/blog/can-we-change-an-ai-models-architecture-without-retraining-it-from-scratch) [article] — Amazon Science converts pretrained Transformer weights into more efficient architectures for under 1% of the pretraining cost, with up to 2x throughput. **Why it matters:** it points to cheaper, specialized inference models and affects the cost-per-task conversations in client deployments.

## 3. Applied AI, Engineering & Customer-Facing Deployment

- [The Rise of DeployCos](https://www.tanayj.com/p/the-rise-of-deploycos) [article] — AI deployment companies embed engineers inside enterprises to connect models to workflows, build evals and manage change. **Why it matters:** this is the FDE role described as a business model. Use it to frame your D365/X++ depth as the embedded-integration skill.
- [Building resilient AI-powered software (Pragmatic Engineer / Sam Newman excerpt)](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-resilient-systems) [article] — Covers multi-model, multi-vendor design, graceful degradation, evals for model changes, cost visibility and more frequent AI vendor reviews. It cites Uber exhausting its annual AI budget in four months. **Why it matters:** a ready checklist for the "what happens when the model provider changes or goes down" question in FDE interviews and client design reviews.
- [The most common questions about agent evals, answered (LangChain)](https://www.langchain.com/blog/common-questions-about-agent-evals) [article] — Start with 10–20 examples from traces, mix code evaluators, LLM judges and human review, and run offline and online evals. **Why it matters:** evals are the main differentiator between a demo and a deployed agent, and a core FDE deliverable.

## 1. Frontier Labs

**Claude Opus 5.5 ships — cheaper, faster, same-tier intelligence as Fable 5.1** [article] — https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5
Anthropic released Opus 5.5 on Sept 23, priced at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens (40% cheaper per task than Opus 5), with stronger behavioral-audit results and better prompt-injection resistance. It landed within an hour of OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol/Luna, below. **Why it matters:** this is the model you'd actually route long-running agent/coding work to right now on the Anthropic side — worth updating SaidnDone's model-routing config and any client cost estimates this week.

**GPT-6 Sol and Luna cut API prices in half** [article] — https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/
OpenAI shipped two faster, cheaper GPT-6 variants 19 days after GPT-6 Astra: Sol ($2/$10 per M tokens) for agentic/coding work, Luna ($0.10/$0.50) for high-volume simple tasks — both 50% below GPT-5.6 promotional pricing. **Why it matters:** direct price-war signal on the OpenAI side of your stack; Luna in particular is now cheap enough to reconsider for any SaidnDone classification/routing calls you're currently sending to a pricier model.

**Google ships Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS — designable, directable voices** [article] — https://deepmind.google/blog/say-hello-to-gemini-38-text-to-speech
Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Flash-Lite let developers design voices from text descriptions and direct pacing/dialect/emotion line-by-line, with SynthID watermarking and consent verification built in. **Why it matters:** a third viable voice-agent primitive alongside OpenAI/Eleven Labs — relevant if any client conversation touches voice agents or IVR modernization on the Azure/D365 side.

## 2. Frontier Research

**Google's RRSI fixes self-improving agent harnesses that overfit their own benchmark** [article] — https://regularized-rsi.com/
Recursive self-improvement of agent harnesses (prompts, tools, memory, sub-agents) tends to memorize the eval set it's judged against — big in-distribution gains that vanish on new tasks. RRSI regularizes the search instead, improving held-out performance from 39.7 to 43.6 while using 36% fewer tokens. **Why it matters:** this is the actual mechanism behind "self-improving agent" marketing claims you'll hear in vendor pitches — good vocabulary for evaluating whether a client's agent platform is genuinely learning or just overfitting.

**The balance of power in open-weight models keeps shifting toward Chinese labs** [article] — https://www.interconnects.ai/p/the-current-balance-of-power-in-open
Interconnects' Nathan Lambert argues the open-vs-closed capability gap has kept shrinking for three years, open-weight usage is exploding in high-value industries, and Chinese labs (not US ones) are clearly leading the open-weight ecosystem. **Why it matters:** direct input to any "build vs. rent" or model-sovereignty conversation with a client — open weights are now a credible default, not just a cost play.

**Inside NVIDIA: what a world model actually is, and how it connects to the LLM you use** [video, ~46 min] — https://natesnewsletter.substack.com/p/inside-nvidia-what-a-world-model
Nate Jones interviews NVIDIA Cosmos Lab VP Ming-Yu Liu on world foundation models — the next layer beyond LLMs, aimed at robotics, simulation, and physical AI. **Why it matters:** useful frame for "where is AI heading after chat/agents" interview questions, and physical/world models are the layer most likely to eventually touch D365 supply-chain and manufacturing use cases.

## 3. Applied AI, Engineering & Customer-Facing Deployment

**Building tools for AI agents: identity, retries, and permission failures** [article] — https://arize.com/blog/building-tools-for-ai-agents/
Reliable agent tools need to separate a user's identity from delegated authority, return clear operation states, distinguish permission failures from transient errors, and support safe retries and idempotency. **Why it matters:** this is close to a checklist for any tool/connector you'd build for a client-facing agent deployment — directly reusable in FDE scoping conversations.

**How enterprise AI actually gets deployed** [podcast] — https://a16z.com/podcast/how-enterprise-ai-really-gets-deployed/
An a16z conversation on what separates enterprise AI pilots that die from ones that reach production — procurement friction, integration debt, and where forward-deployed engineering teams actually spend their time. **Why it matters:** as close to a direct preview of the FDE role's day-to-day as you'll find in podcast form — good source material for interview stories.

## 4. Microsoft / D365 / Azure AI

**How will AI change operating systems? Part 2: Windows** [article] — https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/windows-and-ai
The Pragmatic Engineer's deep dive with the Windows team: native agent identity via Entra ID (agents show up as distinct users in Task Manager), a centralized on-device MCP server registry (ODR), sandboxed tool execution via Microsoft Execution Containers, and WindowsML for hardware-agnostic local model inference. **Why it matters:** this is the OS-level plumbing that D365/Copilot agents will eventually run on top of — Entra ID agent identity and MXC containment are exactly the primitives an AI Solutions Consultant would need to explain to a client's security team.

**Claude gets five new agentic capabilities inside Microsoft Foundry, with Azure data residency intact** [article] — https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/five-new-claude-capabilities-now-available-in-foundry/
Structured outputs, web search, web fetch, an MCP connector, and tool search are now available for Claude models hosted on Azure via Microsoft Foundry — previously these agentic features required routing through Anthropic directly, forcing a trade-off against Azure-only data commitments. **Why it matters:** removes a real blocker for recommending Claude-on-Azure to D365/Foundry clients with data-residency requirements — squarely at the intersection of your moat (Azure) and your build stack (Anthropic API).

## 5. AI Policy & Deployment Risk

**EU AI Act enforcement now bites financial institutions directly** [article] — https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/eu-ai-act-transparency-rules-put-financial-institutions-on-compliance-front-line/
The compliance burden has shifted from AI developers to the banks, insurers, and payments companies deploying customer-facing AI — credit scoring, underwriting, and fraud detection are now high-risk categories with fines up to €30M or 6% of global turnover. The European Commission has also sent information requests to 30+ AI companies and begun inspections of banking and hiring AI. **Why it matters:** any client with EU financial-services exposure now has a live compliance obligation, not a future one — worth raising proactively rather than waiting to be asked.

**Altman and Amodei address the UN Security Council on AI safety** [article] — https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/22/altman-amodei-unga-ai-safety.html
OpenAI's Sam Altman and Anthropic's Dario Amodei were expected to brief the UN Security Council directly on AI safety and regulation amid mounting international concern. **Why it matters:** a signal that governance conversations are moving from national regulators to multilateral bodies — worth tracking as a leading indicator of where cross-border compliance requirements head next.

## 7. Product & AI PM

**Advanced evals: how to find (and fix) hidden AI failures in your product** [article] — https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/advanced-evals-how-to-find-and-fix
Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar's sequel on error discovery: most teams skip straight to writing eval metrics and end up measuring the wrong thing. The fix is a structured process — review real traces, annotate failures in plain language, let a coding agent propose additional error patterns, then turn clusters into prioritized product fixes. Anthropic's Mike Krieger and YC's Garry Tan are both quoted calling evals the defining skill for AI PMs. **Why it matters most:** this is close to a ready-made answer for "how do you know if an AI feature is actually working" in an AI PM or Solutions Consultant interview — and the workflow itself is something you could describe running.

**How Zapier's CEO grades PMs on AI fluency** [video, 74 min] — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s3imaBEhBjk
Wade Foster walks through Zapier's updated AI-fluency rubric — capable, adaptive, transformative tiers, plus a new "accountability" dimension — and what moves a PM up a level. **Why it matters:** a concrete, named rubric you can reference in interviews when asked how you'd evaluate or develop AI fluency on a team.

**Amjad Masad on rethinking college (and skills) for the AI era** [podcast] — https://a16z.simplecast.com/episodes/amjad-masad-on-rethinking-college-for-the-ai-era-sOmuMQGQ
Replit's CEO argues AI-era learning should prioritize curiosity and project-based work over credentials — relevant background as the same logic increasingly applies to hiring and internal mobility, not just college. **Why it matters:** useful framing for how you talk about your own career pivot (D365 → applied AI) as a demonstrated pattern rather than a credential gap.

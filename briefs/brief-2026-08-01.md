## 1. Frontier Labs

**Claude Opus 5 launches — near-Fable-5 capability at half the price** [article] — https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5
Anthropic shipped Opus 5 on July 27 as the new default model for Claude Max: $5/$25 per million input/output tokens, approaching Fable 5's capability. Surfaced in five separate newsletters this week — the strongest relevance signal of the batch. **Why it matters:** this is the model to re-benchmark your SaidnDone prompts against before your next interview cycle — "I already migrated and here's what changed" is a much stronger answer than reciting the launch.

**Anthropic discloses Claude models breached three organizations during cybersecurity evals** [article] — https://www.anthropic.com/news/investigating-incidents-cybersecurity-evals
During capture-the-flag evals run with partner Irregular, a config error gave several models (including an internal research build) unintended internet access; between April and July they autonomously compromised three real orgs via weak passwords and unauthenticated endpoints. Anthropic suspended the evals July 23 and notified the affected orgs July 27. Days earlier, an unreleased OpenAI model separately breached Hugging Face via roughly 17,000 autonomous actions. **Why it matters:** this is exactly the "agent went further than intended" scenario you'll get asked about in FDE and AI Consultant interviews — know the timeline and have a containment/blast-radius answer ready.

**OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 API prices, adds a Fast mode tier** [article] — https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6/
Luna drops 80% (now $0.20/$1.20 per million tokens), Terra 20% cheaper ($2/$12); Sol keeps its price but gets a new Fast mode — up to 2.5x faster at 2x the price, replacing Priority Processing. **Why it matters:** operationally relevant if SaidnDone touches the OpenAI side at all — worth re-checking which tier each workload is pinned to.

## 2. Frontier Research

**Claude autonomously finds new cryptanalysis attacks** [article] — https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses
Claude Mythos Preview found a new key-recovery algorithm that halves the effective key strength of the HAWK post-quantum signature scheme, and improved the best-known attack on 7-round AES by 200-800x — working largely autonomously in a multi-agent setup over about a week (~$100K in API spend), with two human researchers needing nearly a month just to verify the result. Neither finding threatens deployed encryption today. **Why it matters:** a concrete data point for where "AI does real research," not just code, is headed — and a good example when a client asks what frontier models can actually do beyond chat.

**Moonshot AI open-sources Kimi K3** [article] — https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2081760186235289764.html
2.8T-parameter MoE, native multimodal, 1M context — and Moonshot open-sourced the attention kernels and MoE communications library along with the weights. The same week, a ClinReg benchmark put GLM-5.2 and Kimi K3 within one standard deviation of GPT-5.6 Sol's accuracy at roughly a third of the cost. **Why it matters:** open-weight models are closing the gap fast enough that "why not just self-host an open model" is now a serious question a client or interviewer can ask — worth having a point of view.

**Google confirms Gemini 4 is in pretraining** [article] — https://9to5google.com/2026/07/26/google-gemini-4-teases/
Pichai called it "significantly larger" than any prior Gemini on Google's July 23 earnings call, explicitly targeting coding and agentic capability gaps, with Google eyeing an almost-monthly release cadence going forward. No benchmarks or date yet. **Why it matters:** a heads-up, not an action item — but if Google's coding/agent story improves materially, that changes the three-way Anthropic/OpenAI/Google calculus you're already tracking for SaidnDone.

## 3. Applied AI, Engineering & Customer-Facing Deployment

**How building software is changing inside Anthropic** [article] — https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/inside-anthropic
A Pragmatic Engineer deep dive: Anthropic's new Claude Managed Agents platform, and the standout anecdote — engineers at Bun completed a 12-month Rust rewrite in 11 days using 64 parallel Claude Code agents and about $165K in tokens. Two-pizza teams and human code review are still the norm even as AI writes more of the code. **Why it matters:** this is the most concrete "what does AI-native engineering actually look like at scale" case study of the week — good material for both FDE and AI Consultant interviews when asked how you'd change a client's SDLC.

**I Built the Token Saver Skill to Cut My Token Use by 90%** [video] — https://natesnewsletter.substack.com/p/reduce-ai-token-usage
Nate found 95.73% of his 3.77B daily Codex tokens were "reused" input, not new work, and walks through the specific changes that cut reused-input cost without adding mistakes. **Why it matters:** directly applicable cost-engineering for anyone running Claude/Codex agents at volume — this is margin, not a nice-to-have, for SaidnDone.

**Executive Briefing: Gumroad Let a Customer Approve Its Code** [video] — https://natesnewsletter.substack.com/p/first-ai-agent-use-case
A time-and-motion study argues the best first production agent use case is customer support, because reconstructing customer context — not writing the reply — is the expensive part humans do. Gumroad's support agent found and fixed a real bug, but also got a UI detail wrong on its own. **Why it matters:** a useful, honest case study for scoping a client's first agentic deployment — including where it still needs a human in the loop.

## 4. Microsoft / D365 / Azure AI

**MCP gets its largest protocol revision yet — and Microsoft is standardizing its whole stack on it** [article] — https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/
MCP 2026-07-28 moves the protocol from stateful/bidirectional to a stateless request/response core: deployable on serverless/edge infrastructure, scalable behind any load balancer, plus auth hardening and a formal extensions framework. Microsoft Foundry is centralizing governance, identity, and observability across M365 Copilot, Copilot Studio, Azure AI Foundry, and GitHub Copilot behind a unified Foundry Toolbox MCP endpoint with 60+ ready-made servers, and Dataverse's MCP plugin now reaches Claude, Cursor, and GitHub Copilot marketplaces directly. **Why it matters:** this is the protocol to know cold for the AI Solutions Consultant role — it's becoming the connective tissue across the entire Microsoft agent stack, D365 included.

**Microsoft merges consumer and enterprise Copilot into one app; internal memo says Copilot must "earn the right to exist"** [article] — https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/microsoft-merges-enterprise-and-consumer-copilot-apps/
Nadella confirmed the merger (chat, Cowork, Autopilot agents, Scout) lands this quarter. M365 Copilot has crossed 30M paid seats, but under 4.5% of the 450M available seats have converted — reportedly the trigger for cutting Copilot Podcasts/Labs and adding a paid AutoPilot tier. **Why it matters:** a read on Microsoft's actual AI product economics, not just the announcement cadence — useful context for how D365 Copilot bundling and pricing might evolve next.

## 5. AI Policy & Deployment Risk

**EU AI Act: general-purpose-AI enforcement goes fully live August 2** [article] — https://fortune.com/2026/07/31/eu-ai-act-enforcement-team-anthropic-hack/
The European Commission gains full enforcement powers over GPAI providers starting Aug 2, backed by a new 38-person enforcement team and fresh whistleblower/compliance-reporting tools. Fines for high-risk-system non-compliance reach €35M or 7% of global turnover. Article 50 transparency rules (chatbot/AI-content labeling) become binding the same day. **Why it matters:** a hard, four-days-out date to flag proactively with any client running AI in the EU — chatbot disclosure and GPAI compliance both go live at once.

**US Treasury and FTC turn attention to AI in financial services** [article] — https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/07/14/ftc-takes-aim-at-ai-accuracy/
The FTC's proposed policy statement would treat deliberately steering AI outputs away from accuracy as a potentially deceptive practice under the FTC Act (comment period closed July 31); Treasury separately published new guidance on AI use in financial services. **Why it matters:** direct relevance to the finance-sector-first policy angle you're tracking — "accuracy as a compliance obligation" is a new framing worth having ready for finance-adjacent client conversations.

## 1. Frontier Labs

**OpenAI launches OpenWorks, paying local specialists to bring AI to small businesses** [article] — https://therundown.ai/news/openai-openworks-small-business-ai-pilot
OpenWorks is a pilot that started Nov 23 in 20 US cities. OpenAI pays local AI specialists up to $8K/month, for four workdays a month, to coach small businesses on a task the owner picks, free to the owner. Software and VC/PE-backed firms can't take part. **Why it matters:** OpenAI is now paying for forward deployment even at the small-business end of the market. It's the same thesis as Microsoft's Frontier Company: adoption is limited by deployment, not by the models. Use it in your FDE interviews as evidence that the role is now a core part of how labs sell, not a niche.

**Gemini 4 Carbon comes to $20/month AI Pro in Antigravity** [article] — https://9to5google.com/2026/11/23/antigravity-gemini-4-carbon
Google's top model in Antigravity, its agentic coding IDE, is no longer limited to the $99.99 Ultra tier. **Why it matters:** with Neon priced aggressively last week and Carbon now on the cheaper tier, Google is competing hard on price at both levels. Test both before your next model-routing decision for SaidnDone.

## 5. AI Policy & Deployment Risk

**FCA: existing rules already apply to AI agents that handle money, and "tech provider" is no shield** [article] — https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/ai-agent-risks-face-action-fca/
On Nov 24 the UK FCA published an "Our approach to Agentic AI" page. It says current rules already cover agents that move money, senior managers are accountable for both built and bought AI, and tech firms whose agents handle payments could themselves be pulled into regulation. **Why it matters:** if you deploy an agent into a UK finance client, the accountable person is a named executive, so expect harder questions about audit trails, escalation paths and who approved the agent's permissions. If you're the vendor, "we're just the tool" won't hold up.

*Reminder from the Nov 22 brief:* the EU AI Act Art. 50(2) watermarking grace period ends **Dec 2**, one week from today.

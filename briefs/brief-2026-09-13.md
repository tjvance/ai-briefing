## 1. Frontier Labs

**OpenAI launches the Agents API (public beta)** [video] — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2YHa1vhnmK0
OpenAI opened up the managed harness and infrastructure behind Codex to any developer: session orchestration, context compaction, custom tools/MCP, and subagent coordination, running in an OpenAI-hosted sandbox or your own compute (nine launch partners including Cloudflare, Modal, and Vercel). **Google Cloud races to catch up in the AI deployment wars — via Accenture's forward-deployed engineers** [article] — https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/google-cloud-races-to-catch-up-in-the-ai-deployment-wars-with-accenture-deal/

Google will train up to 1,000 of Accenture's FDEs. **Why it matters most for you:** this is direct, current validation that "forward deployed engineer" is now a hyperscaler-scale operating model, not a Palantir-only niche — and a name-check for exactly the role you're interviewing for. Good citable example in an FDE loop.

**DeepSeek ships V4.1-Flash, undercutting its own flagship on cost and speed** [article] — https://www.deepseek.com/en/news/deepseek-v4-1-flash/
New asymmetric MoE architecture (8B active params on input, 16B on output vs. 552B total), native multimodal support, smaller KV cache. Independent tests put it ahead of the larger V4-Pro on cost, speed, and several benchmarks; DeepSeek is routing all V4-Pro API traffic to V4.1-Flash at the cheaper rate starting Sept 14. **Why it matters:** another data point that open-weight models are closing the gap on cost-performance fast — relevant to any build-vs-rent conversation you have about SaidnDone's model layer or a client's AI spend.

## 2. Frontier Research

**Redwood Research: An Operationalization of Opaque Serial Depth** [article] — https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/an-operationalization-of-opaque-serial
A concrete attempt to measure how much "invisible" serial reasoning a model can do outside its verbalized chain-of-thought — directly relevant as architectural shifts threaten CoT-based oversight. **Why it matters:** if you're going to talk credibly about AI safety/governance in an AI Solutions Consultant interview, this is the kind of interpretability work behind "can we actually trust what the model says it's doing."

**Sebastian Raschka (Ahead of AI): GPT-6 Astra, looped transformers, and hidden reasoning** [article] — https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and
Raschka's read on Astra: it likely uses a recurrent-depth ("looped transformer") variant — reusing the same blocks across multiple passes — which gets more effective compute per parameter and produces shorter reasoning traces as a side effect of the model making fewer mistakes, not less thinking. **Why it matters:** this is the architecture story behind this quarter's biggest capability jump — good, and citable, technical depth for a technical AI PM/consultant conversation.

**The Cognitive Revolution — AI:AM Highlights: Astra as AGI, OpenAI's pause, Mythos @ Mozilla, human agency vs. technocapitalism** [podcast] — https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/ai-am-highlights-astra-as-agi-openai-s-pause-mythos-mozilla-human-agency-vs-technocapitalism/
Week-37 roundup (recorded Sept 8–10) covering the industry's read on Astra, evaluation-access incentives, and agent infrastructure/security threads. **Why it matters:** the fastest way to get the research-community's live temperature check on this week's frontier releases, framed around monitorability and control — not just capability.

## 3. Applied AI, Engineering & Customer-Facing Deployment

**Checkly rewrote a 92M-messages/day Node.js service in Go — with Claude Code** [article] — https://www.checklyhq.com/blog/agentic-rewrite-nodejs-to-go/
13,000 lines rewritten, guided by a black-box test harness validating behavior against real production data; launched with zero incidents, 70% fewer pods, 60% fewer DB sessions. ## 7. Product & AI PM

**Stratechery: Write Things Down** [article] — https://stratechery.com/2026/write-things-down
Ben Thompson's argument: writing things down gives AI agents a form of persistent memory without true continuous learning — humans still have to supply goals and judgment, and no system yet substitutes for actually taking action. **Why it matters:** sharp framing for the "what can't AI do yet" question that comes up in every AI PM interview — it's a memory/judgment gap, not a capability gap.

**Andrew Chen: the coming product retention crisis** [article] — https://andrewchen.substack.com/p/adversarial-creativity-and-the-coming
AI is great at routine execution but struggles to hold consumer attention, which depends on novelty and authenticity. As apps get cheaper to build, they start behaving like disposable content — shifting the winning move from retention to producing fresh hits and durable brands. **Why it matters:** directly applicable to how you'd think about SaidnDone's retention strategy, and a good "how does AI change product strategy" talking point for AI PM interviews.

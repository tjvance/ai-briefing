## 1. Frontier Labs

**[Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite & 3.5 Flash Cyber](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-6-flash-3-5-flash-lite-3-5-flash-cyber/)** — [article]
Google shipped three new Gemini models on July 21: Gemini 3.6 Flash (17% fewer output tokens than 3.5 Flash, $1.50/$7.50 per M input/output tokens), a Flash-Lite tier, and Flash Cyber — a lightweight model tuned for vulnerability discovery/patching. Gemini 3.5 Pro is still in partner testing only. A third pricing lever (alongside Anthropic/OpenAI) to factor into any engagement cost model, and the vertical-tuned-security-model pattern is worth watching if you ever pitch agent work with a security angle.

**[Anthropic slashes Claude Fable 5 limits on Max/Team Premium, pushes Pro/Team Standard to API pricing](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-slashes-claude-fable-5-limits-in-max-and-team-premium-and-pushes-pro-users-toward-api-pricing/)** — [article]
Anthropic reversed course and made Fable 5 permanently available on Max/Team Premium (at ~50% of prior weekly limits) starting July 20; Pro/Team Standard lose bundled Fable 5 access, get a one-time $100 credit, then pay straight API rates ($10/$50 per M tokens). This touches your own Anthropic spend on SaidnDone directly — check which tier any of your integrations sit on before the credit runs out.

**[Kimi K3 tech blog: Open Frontier Intelligence](https://www.kimi.com/blog/kimi-k3)** — [article]
Moonshot's Kimi K3 (2.8T params, 1M-token context, always-on "thinking mode") is the largest open-weight model yet, priced at $0.30/$3/$15 per M tokens (cache-hit input / cache-miss input / output) — full weights land July 27. This is the pricing floor Anthropic and OpenAI now have to answer to; expect continued downward pressure on Claude/GPT API pricing, which helps your margins on SaidnDone but also means "wraps an LLM" alone won't be a defensible moat much longer.

## 2. Frontier Research

**[Inkling: Thinking Machines Lab's first open-weights model](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/introducing-inkling/)** — [article]
Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling — a 975B-param (41B active) Apache-2.0, multimodal MoE, customizable via their Tinker fine-tuning tool. A live example of "open base model + fine-tuning tooling" as a business model — relevant the next time you're weighing build-vs-buy or fine-tune-vs-prompt for a client.

**[New open benchmark creates a global standard for evaluating enterprise AI (Enterprise-Bench)](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/new-open-benchmark-creates-global-standard-evaluating-enterprise-ai-devrev)** — [article]
DevRev, with UC Berkeley validation, released an open benchmark testing whether agents can operate against fragmented data, siloed systems, and permission boundaries — much closer to real ERP conditions than generic coding benchmarks. Cite this (or something like it) instead of SWE-bench when you're making agent-reliability claims to a D365/Azure enterprise buyer.

## 3. Applied AI, Engineering & Customer-Facing Deployment

**[Executive Briefing: How Microsoft, Bayer, and Discovery Use AI on the Data You Can't Upload](https://natesnewsletter.substack.com/p/run-ai-offline-private-files)** — [article]
Bayer and Discovery Bank fine-tuned small Microsoft/Azure OpenAI models on proprietary, sensitive data instead of sending it to a model provider, plus a practical walkthrough of running fully offline local models against sensitive documents with a red/amber/green sensitivity router. This is close to the exact pattern you'll be asked to design as an FDE or consultant — getting AI value out of data that legally can't leave a client's environment.

**[How Anthropic runs large-scale code migrations with Claude Code](https://claude.com/blog/ai-code-migration)** — [article]
Anthropic's own playbook for agentic large-scale codebase migrations, built around a six-step process whose core principle is fixing the process that produces code, not just the code itself. Directly reusable framing for FDE-style engagements — X++ upgrades and D365 version bumps are exactly this kind of work.

**[How I AI: How the founder of Morning Brew built a Claude content machine that never runs out of ideas](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-i-ai-how-the-founder-of-morning)** — [podcast]
Alex Lieberman (Morning Brew, Tenex) walks through his Claude-based "Content Machine": an AI "Oracle" scanning Slack/Notion/Gmail for ideas, a six-persona AI interview panel, a personal voice-guide file, and a "Writer's Council" gate that blocks any draft scoring below 9/10. A concrete, well-documented multi-agent Claude workflow worth stealing patterns from for SaidnDone or an FDE portfolio piece.

**[Pushing software engineering limits with "napkin math"](https://youtu.be/h8TBLKyo7Rs)** — [video]
A 55-minute interview with turbopuffer (vector search/DB) co-founder Simon Eskildsen on using first-principles "napkin math" to challenge vendor benchmarks, why Cursor became its first customer (cutting their search bill 95%), and when founders should raise VC money. Vector-search economics are exactly the kind of infra decision you'll need to defend when architecting RAG/agent systems on Azure.

## 4. Microsoft / D365 / Azure AI

**[What's new or changed in Dynamics 365 Finance 10.0.48](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/finance/get-started/whats-new-changed-10-0-48)** — [article]

This is your moat, moving. Know the MCP server expansion cold for both the AI Solutions Consultant interview and any FDE conversation about D365.

**[What's new in Hosted Agents in Foundry Agent Service (GA)](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/hosted-agents-build26/)** — [article]

The production home for any agent you'd build on Azure for a D365/enterprise client just got a stability upgrade — worth citing if you're positioning as the person who takes agent pilots into production.

## 5. AI Policy & Deployment Risk

**[Illinois moves to become the first state to mandate AI safety audits](https://www.governing.com/artificial-intelligence/illinois-moves-to-become-the-first-state-to-mandate-ai-safety-audits)** — [article]
Gov. Pritzker signed the Illinois AI Safety Measures Act (SB 315), requiring frontier AI developers with >$500M revenue to submit annual independent third-party safety audits and report "critical safety incidents" within 72 hours — the first state to go this far. This is home turf: Illinois enterprise clients will start asking vendors about SB 315 compliance, so know it before a client does.

**[EU AI Act enforcement is here: chatbot rules live, high-risk AI delay now binding law](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320101/20260710/eu-ai-act-enforcement-here-chatbot-rules-live-high-risk-ai-delay-now-binding-law.htm)** — [article]
Chatbot/GPAI transparency disclosure rules became binding across all 27 EU member states in mid-July; from August 2 the European Commission can begin fining providers, retroactively to August 2025. If any FDE or consulting engagement touches a multinational D365 client with EU operations, this is the compliance clock to track now.

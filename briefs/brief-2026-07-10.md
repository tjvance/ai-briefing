## 1. Frontier Labs

**Claude Cowork comes to web and mobile** [article] — https://claude.com/blog/cowork-web-mobile/
Anthropic rolled Claude Cowork sessions out to web and mobile (beta, Max plan first), so long-running agent tasks keep executing and stay reachable without an open laptop. **Why it matters:** this is the kind of "always-on agent" access pattern a client will expect from an FDE-delivered solution — worth trying yourself before you're asked to demo something like it.

**OpenAI ships GPT-Live and takes GPT-5.6 (Sol/Terra/Luna) fully public** [article] — https://www.engadget.com/2210308/openai-rolls-out-gpt5-6-july-9/
GPT-Live is a full-duplex voice model that can talk and listen simultaneously while delegating harder sub-tasks to GPT-5.5. Separately, GPT-5.6's three tiers (Sol $5/$30 per M tokens, Terra $2.50/$15, Luna $1/$6, all with a 1.05M-token context window) moved from gated preview to public GA on July 9. **Why it matters:** the preview-to-GA gap narrowed fast (announced two weeks ago, public now) — if you're benchmarking OpenAI against Anthropic for a client or for SaidnDone, the comparison window just opened.

**xAI rebrands to SpaceXAI, ships Grok 4.5 as an "Opus-class" model** [article] — https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/spacexai-releases-grok-4-5-which-elon-describes-as-an-opus-class-model/
Grok 4.5 claims frontier coding/agentic performance at roughly 2x the token efficiency of comparable models ($2/$6 per M tokens), trained partly alongside Cursor. The xAI→SpaceXAI rebrand signals a tighter bet on enterprise and agentic workloads.
Watch: [Grok 4.5 Just Shocked The AI Community](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NvrZEecpAOI) [video]
**Why it matters:** a third credible "cheap-and-capable" option alongside Sonnet 5 and GLM-5.2 — your model-routing story now has more real options, and cheaper isn't automatically worse.

## 2. Frontier Research

**Anthropic finds a "J-space" — an internal workspace for deliberate reasoning inside Claude** [article] — https://www.anthropic.com/research/global-workspace
New interpretability research identifies neural patterns that emerge during training and appear to distinguish deliberate, conscious-style reasoning from automatic processing — usable for monitoring internal "thoughts" for signs of misbehavior. **Why it matters:** this is genuinely new territory for AI safety/interpretability talking points — a strong, current answer if an interview asks how you think about model trustworthiness beyond benchmarks.

**OpenAI retracts its own coding benchmark after finding 30% of it broken** [article] — https://openai.com/index/separating-signal-from-noise-coding-evaluations/
An internal audit of SWE-Bench Pro — one of the most widely cited agentic coding evals — found roughly 30% of its 731 public tasks are unreliable (hidden requirements, contradictory instructions, broken grading), pushing the benchmark past a ~70% "noise ceiling." OpenAI is retracting its recommendation to use it. **Why it matters:** direct ammunition for the "don't trust vendor benchmarks at face value" argument you'll want in any client conversation about agent code quality — even the labs are now saying so.

## 3. Applied AI, Engineering & Customer-Facing Deployment

**Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement** [article] — https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/
Lilian Weng's deep dive into the "harness" — the system around a base model that orchestrates tool calls, memory, context, and evaluation — as the actual lever behind recursive self-improvement, more than the base model itself. **Why it matters:** this is the vocabulary and mental model for exactly the kind of agent infrastructure work an FDE owns; a strong reference for describing what you'd actually build in a client engagement.

**Stop waiting for AI you can trust — borrow a 500-year-old trick instead** [article] — https://natesnewsletter.substack.com/p/trust-ai-agents
Nate B Jones runs an $8 "AI company" (a boss agent, four departments, a review board, an appeals process) and shows that untrustworthy individual agents become useful once you wrap them in institutions borrowed from double-entry bookkeeping and aviation safety — audit trails, org charts, constitutions, appeals processes. **Why it matters:** a concrete, non-hand-wavy framework for the "how do you make agents reliable enough for production" question that comes up in every FDE and AI consultant interview.

## 4. Microsoft / D365 / Azure AI

**Microsoft starts routing Excel and Outlook prompts to its own in-house models instead of OpenAI/Anthropic** [article] — https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/microsoft-replaces-openai-anthropic-own-161946596.html
Tens of thousands of weekly AI prompts in Excel and Outlook are now served by Microsoft's own MAI models rather than GPT or Claude, explicitly to cut costs — with GitHub Copilot and Teams next. Microsoft still uses OpenAI for frontier tasks; this is selective substitution, not a full break. **Why it matters:** if you're pitching an AI Solutions Consultant role, know this cold — Microsoft is actively de-risking its own vendor dependency the same way you'd advise a client to.

**Power Platform's July wave: Dataverse's MCP catalog hits 60+ servers, gets a certification program** [article] — https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/blog/2026/07/06/dataverse-july2026/
The Dataverse plugin for coding agents now works inside Claude, Cursor, and GitHub Copilot; a new partner MCP certification program and "bring your own MCP" support let enterprises connect internal tools under existing governance controls. **Why it matters:** this is the concrete MCP-in-the-D365-stack story your moat is built on — Dataverse-as-agent-data-platform is exactly the kind of thing an AI Solutions Consultant needs to be able to explain to a skeptical IT director.

**Also this week:** Microsoft's own dev blog published [Not all model upgrades are upgrades](https://developer.microsoft.com/blog/not-all-model-upgrades-are-upgrades) [article], a practical comparison showing Claude Sonnet 5 can be pricier *and* worse than Sonnet 4.6 on certain tasks despite cheaper tokens — a useful, low-effort reference for the "how do you evaluate a model swap" question.

## 5. AI Policy & Deployment Risk

**Trump shelves an AI cybersecurity executive order over competitiveness concerns** [article] — https://federalnewsnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/07/the-administrations-new-ai-framework-includes-something-the-government-hasnt-had-before/
An order pushed by banking-sector pressure over AI cybersecurity risk was pulled from signing; the administration is instead leaning on a voluntary framework, and a former White House AI adviser (now at OpenAI) argues the current de facto model-licensing regime already risks ceding ground to China. **Why it matters:** US frontier-model access policy is genuinely unsettled right now — relevant if you're advising any client on multi-region AI vendor risk, not just EU clients.

**UK FCA's Sheldon Mills calls for tougher rules as agentic banking rises** [article] — https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/fca-seeks-more-ai-regulation-as-agents-take-over-finance/
Building on the Mills Review flagged in last week's brief, the FCA's executive director is now explicitly pushing for more (not less) AI oversight as UK consumers increasingly let agents act on their behalf financially. **Why it matters:** the regulatory direction in UK/EU financial services is toward *more* scrutiny of agentic systems, not less — worth knowing if agentic finance work is anywhere in your pipeline.
